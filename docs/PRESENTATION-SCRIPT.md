# ValueGram Profit: presentation script (v2, for approval)

**Audience:** enterprise executives and investors
**Length:** 24 slides, about 20 minutes
**Language:** English. **Currency:** USD.

**Through-line:** *Activity is not value.* Organizations measure what got done. ValueGram Profit measures what it was worth, before you commit and after you deliver.

**Signature visual: the ValueGram.** A single living line, like an ECG of economic value, runs through the whole deck. It pulses when value is created, flatlines when work creates none, and jumps when connected decisions unlock each other. It opens the deck, carries the big reveal on slide 6, and closes it.

**Capability tags** (small, consistent, on every slide where the distinction matters):
- `LIVE`: implemented in the product today
- `OPTIONAL`: an analytical layer that runs when enabled
- `ROADMAP`: proposed direction, not a delivery commitment

**Disclosure rule:** the deck names disciplines, method families, inputs, outputs and evidence. It never shows formulas, parameters, thresholds, mappings, schemas, vendor or library names, or model identifiers. These speaker notes ship with the deck, so they follow the same rule.

## Prologue

### 01 · Title

> **ValueGram Profit**
> Every decision has a price.
> Now you can see it before you choose.

**Build:** the ValueGram line draws across a dark field, pulses once, and the product name resolves out of it.
**Notes:** Every organization spends three things: time, money and attention. Today it sees the cost of all three and almost never the return. This is about making that return visible.

## Act I · The blind spot

### 02 · Statement

> Organizations measure **what got done**.
> Almost none measure **what it was worth**.

**Build:** line 1, pause, line 2 in gold.
**Notes:** Dashboards report completion, velocity and status. None of them answer the question a CFO asks: what did this change in profit?

### 03 · The completed plan

> **Plan delivered: 100%.**
> **Profit change: $0.**
>
> Which improvement actually changes profit?
> What has to happen before it pays?
> When does more effort stop creating value?

**Build:** a task board fills to 100% and turns green; beside it the ValueGram stays flat. Then the three questions appear one at a time.
**Tag:** illustrative.
**Notes:** A team can finish everything it promised while another part of the business blocks the benefit from reaching the P&L. Completion tells you something happened. It does not tell you it mattered. Keep this picture in mind; we'll come back to it.

## Act II · The shift

### 04 · Goals become economic cases

> Goals stop being targets.
> **They become economic cases.**

Each goal carries:
- **Its value:** what the next improvement is worth, and what the full target is worth
- **Its dependencies:** what must happen first for it to pay
- **Its stopping point:** where more effort stops creating value
- **Its evidence:** how strong the numbers behind it are

**Build:** one goal card flips and reveals its four layers in turn.
**Tag:** `LIVE` (for goals linked to the current industry model)
**Notes:** Supporting goals still matter even when they carry no direct dollar value. The system classifies them as enablers instead of pretending to price them.

### 05 · Value lives at the constraint

> Value lives at the constraint.
> **Improve what isn't holding you back, and you create nothing. Yet.**

**Build:** work flows as particles through two gates, Capacity and Approvals. The narrower gate glows. Widen the other one: nothing changes. Widen the glowing one: the flow rises until the second gate starts to glow.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** A business that can deliver more than it can approve gains nothing from more capacity. Speed up approvals and the same capacity becomes valuable. Priorities depend on the state of the whole business, and that state moves.

### 06 · The reveal

> **Two goals. $119,600 together.**

| Modeled change | Added operating profit per year |
|---|---:|
| Raise utilization (77% → 82%) alone | **$58,400** |
| Cut approval time (37 → 20 days) alone | **$0** |
| Both together | **$119,600** |

> **$61,200 exists only when they move together.**
> A zero doesn't mean worthless. **It means "not yet".**

**Build:** (1) utilization: the ValueGram rises. (2) approvals: it flatlines at $0. Hold the pause. (3) together: it jumps far past the sum. (4) the $61,200 interaction value lights up. (5) callback caption: *"Remember the plan that delivered 100% and changed nothing? That was the approvals team."*
**On-slide disclosure (always visible):** Illustrative ship-repair scenario from the product's engine tests. Modeled annual uplift, not a customer result. Implementation costs excluded.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** On its own, faster approval appears worthless because capacity is the binding limit. Spreadsheets and scorecards would have cut it. The engine shows it is the key that unlocks the other improvement. The decision is to coordinate the two teams, not to defund one of them.

### 07 · Every improvement has a stopping point

> Every improvement has a stopping point.
> **Knowing where it is changes the target.**

- Each extra point of utilization is worth **$29,200 a year**, up to **79%**.
- Past 79%, approvals become the limit and the next point is worth nothing.
- Deliver faster approvals as well, and value keeps growing to **81.1%**.

**Build:** a value curve climbs, hits a knee at 79% and goes flat. Add the second goal: the knee slides right to 81.1%.
**Tag:** `LIVE`; same illustrative scenario.
**Notes:** This is why the system suggests targets. A target beyond its stopping point spends effort that returns nothing. A target set below it leaves value on the table.

### 08 · Deal Desk

> A good margin can still be **a bad deal**.

Every deal consumes capacity. When capacity is scarce, it displaces business you already had.
Deal Desk prices a deal against what it displaces and returns:
- **The modeled price floor:** the lowest price at which it still adds value
- **The incremental value:** what the business gains, net of what it gives up

**Build:** a new deal enters a capacity bar and pushes an existing block out. The floor price appears.
**Tag:** `LIVE` (for the configured industry model)
**Notes:** When there is idle capacity, nothing is displaced and the floor is close to variable cost. When capacity is full, the deal must pay for the business it pushes out.

### 09 · Monday List

> Monday morning.
> **An agenda written in dollars.**

- Which constraint deserves attention this week?
- Which decision is waiting, and what does waiting cost?
- Which uncertain number could change the answer?

**Build:** a short ranked list. The top item expands to show value at stake, the pending decision and the weak input.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** The weekly cost-of-delay figure is an urgency signal derived from annual value. It is not a cash-flow forecast. The point is to start the leadership meeting on what matters most.

### 10 · Talk to the economics

> *"What if approvals took 20 days?"*
> **The engine calculates. The AI explains. Leaders decide.**

**Build:** the question is typed. It turns into a structured scenario, the engine recalculates, and a plain-language answer appears with its number traced back to the calculation.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** The AI does not invent the economics. It turns the question into a structured change, the financial engine recomputes the scenario, and the AI explains the computed result. The same facts feed proposed task plans, which a person approves before anything is created.

### 11 · Value Ledger

> Forecasts promise.
> **The ledger keeps score.**

Candidate → Baseline → Proving → **Proven** or **Missed**

- The baseline locks the starting point before work begins.
- Recorded financial results test the claim.
- Credited value can never exceed the improvement the books recorded. **No double counting.**

**Build:** one improvement travels along the track. Three initiatives then try to claim the same gain, and the ledger caps their total at what was actually recorded.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** "Proven" means the improvement passed the product's evaluation criteria. It is not a claim of universal causal proof, because results are always influenced by more than one initiative. Demonstration entries are kept apart from real ones.

### 12 · One economic language

> One economic language
> **across the leadership table.**

| Role | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| CEO | Where should management attention go? |
| CFO | Which assumptions support this value claim? |
| COO | Which constraint should operations attack next? |
| Commercial | Which deals make the best use of capacity? |
| Strategy | Which goals depend on one another? |

**Build:** one decision at the center; five perspectives connect to it one by one.
**Notes:** People will still disagree. Now they disagree about visible assumptions and constraints instead of opinions.

## Act III · Under the hood

### 13 · The architecture

> Five disciplines.
> **One decision architecture.**

| Discipline | Its role |
|---|---|
| **Financial modeling** | Represents how operations turn into profit |
| **Mathematical optimization** | Finds the best feasible outcome and prices every constraint |
| **Statistics** | Forecasts, measures impact and scores its own accuracy |
| **Artificial intelligence** | Interprets intent and explains computed results |
| **Evidence and data** | Preserves every observation, assumption, decision and outcome |

**Build (system animation, 5 steps):** business data enters → the financial model forms → the optimizer finds the constraint → AI translates for people → outcomes flow back into the evidence base. The loop closes and the ValueGram pulses.
**Notes:** Each discipline answers a different part of the management question. The value is in how they are connected, from question to computed scenario to decision to measured outcome.

### 14 · The financial mathematics

> The mathematics of **the next move**.

- **Constrained optimization:** the best achievable result under real limits
- **Marginal valuation:** what one more unit of a scarce resource is worth
- **Constraint-shift detection:** where the bottleneck moves next
- **Interaction analysis:** value that exists only when goals move together
- **Opportunity cost:** what a decision displaces
- **Accounting reconciliation:** modeled figures checked against the books

**Build:** a value surface with a visible ridge where one constraint hands over to the next. Abstract and accurate, never the product equations.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** These are established mathematical techniques. We don't claim to have invented them. What is new is connecting them to enterprise goals, deals, evidence and outcome evaluation, so that a goal arrives with its economic case. Results hold within the stated business model.

### 15 · Statistical evidence

> Every forecast must **beat the simple answer**.

- **Baseline challenge:** an advanced forecast is used only if it beats a simple seasonal benchmark on history
- **Ranges, not points:** forecasts come with an uncertainty band
- **Self-scoring:** accuracy, bias and coverage of past predictions are measured
- **Impact analysis:** results are compared with the pre-intervention pattern
- **Stress tests:** placebo dates, random variables and data subsets try to break every impact claim

**Build:** a forecast band runs forward; the real outcome is drawn over it; a score appears.
**Tag:** `LIVE` (core scoring) · `OPTIONAL` (analytical layer)
**Notes:** Robustness checks test assumptions. They don't replace sound study design. When the analytical layer is offline, the core profit engine keeps running.

### 16 · Grounded AI

> **No number without a source.**

- **Grounded context:** the AI sees the relevant goals, links, risks and computed facts
- **Structured intent:** plain-language questions become precise scenario changes
- **Validated output:** answer structure, references and figures are checked against what was supplied
- **Human approval:** proposed plans become tasks only after someone approves them
- **Governed agent access:** external AI agents work through permissioned tools, not open data

**Build:** a question flows through a grounding layer and a validation gate. One unsupported figure is stopped at the gate.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** The validation catches unsupported figures. It does not guarantee every sentence of an explanation is correct, which is why the number and its source are always shown together.

### 17 · Every number knows where it came from

> Every number knows **where it came from**.

**Five grades of evidence:** estimate → stated → sampled → system record → controlled test

- Append-only history: nothing is overwritten
- Every calculation is fingerprinted and versioned, so it can be reproduced
- Stronger evidence beats weaker; newer beats older at the same grade

> **The decision record becomes an asset.**
> What we believed. What we expected. What we chose. What happened.

**Build:** the ladder climbs; then a single decision record collects evidence over time.
**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** Evidence grades are recorded classifications; they show where a number came from rather than independently certifying it. Over time, complete records of beliefs, predictions, decisions and outcomes become a dataset for better calibration. That is an opportunity being built, not a corpus we claim to have today.

### 18 · Built to earn trust

> Run it in the shadows.
> **Switch it on when it has earned trust.**

- **Off → Shadow → On:** adopt gradually; in Shadow it calculates without changing anyone's priorities
- **Adjustable influence:** leaders set how strongly economics weighs on rankings
- **Books first:** large gaps between model and accounting raise warnings and errors
- **Demo never mixes with real:** demonstration data is always separated

**Tag:** `LIVE`
**Notes:** Adoption is a management decision, not a software switch. The product is designed so that trust is built on evidence.

## Act IV · What comes next

### 19 · The value-native enterprise

> Today: goals, deals and the weekly agenda.
> **Tomorrow: every decision carries its economic consequence.**

Every task. Every meeting. Every hire. Every investment.

**Build:** the ValueGram spreads from one goal across the whole organization map.
**Tag:** vision
**Notes:** This is the transformation: value measurement stops being a quarterly finance exercise and becomes the operating language of the company. The next three slides are the technical path.

### 20 · Decisions across uncertain futures

> Choose plans that **still work when the world doesn't cooperate**.

- Connected scenarios for demand, cost and capacity
- Upside weighed against downside exposure
- Plans that stay robust across many possible futures

**Methods:** correlated Monte Carlo scenarios · stochastic and robust optimization · tail-risk measures (CVaR)
**Build:** a fan of possible futures; the robust plan stays inside the safe zone across most of them.
**Tag:** `ROADMAP`
**Notes:** Today the engine recalculates explicit scenarios. Next it reasons across many joint scenarios at once. Distributions must be calibrated on real data before deployment.

### 21 · Capital and time

> From operating profit to **capital allocation**.

- Which initiatives deserve funding?
- In what order should they happen?
- When should the company expand, wait or stop?

**Methods:** multi-period optimization · mixed-integer decisions · discounted cash flow · real-options reasoning
**Build:** initiatives line up on a timeline with funding gates that open or close.
**Tag:** `ROADMAP`
**Notes:** This adds implementation cost, timing, cash flow and budgets to the model. Every extension needs explicit assumptions and finance review.

### 22 · Smarter evidence, every industry

> The system learns **which question is worth asking next**.

- **Multivariate forecasting:** business signals and external drivers forecast together
- **Stronger counterfactuals:** synthetic controls and difference-in-differences
- **Value of information:** measure first where better data would change the decision most
- **Industry models:** a growing library of validated economic models, one per sector

**Methods:** time-series foundation models · Bayesian updating · expected value of information
**Build:** three research tracks converge into an expanding grid of industry models.
**Tag:** `ROADMAP`
**Notes:** One industry model is live today, on an architecture built to take more. Each new sector needs domain expertise, financial reconciliation and pilot evidence.

## Finale

### 23 · The pilot

> Start with **one decision you can measure**.

One business process · a clear financial baseline · a few connected goals · one recorded decision and outcome

**We measure:** time to a supported decision · changes to priorities and targets · forecast calibration · realized improvement against the agreed baseline

**Build:** a single path from baseline to measured outcome.
**Notes:** Agree the baseline, scope, measurement period and success criteria before any claim is made. Targets are set together with the pilot partner. Financial validation can take longer than onboarding.

### 24 · Close

> Activity is not value.
> **ValueGram Profit measures the difference.**
>
> See what it's worth. Decide with evidence. Prove what it delivered.

**Build:** the ValueGram line returns, pulses and settles. The product name resolves, followed by one invitation: *Choose the decision for your pilot.*
**Notes:** Invite enterprise leaders to name a process for a measured pilot. With investors, talk about what it takes to validate repeatable deployments and expand the industry library. No invented traction, customers or funding terms.
