Step 0: work flows left to right through two limits, approvals and capacity. The glowing gate is the one that binds. Only work that passes it turns gold. Step 1: raise utilization from 77% to 82%: more gold, the heartbeat starts, $58,400 a year, and the binding limit moves to approvals. Step 2: back to today, and instead cut approvals from 37 to 20 days. More work gets through the first gate and piles up at capacity. Nothing new turns gold. Flatline. Pause here; let the silence work. Step 3: move both: $119,600. Step 4: $61,200 of that exists only in the combination. A spreadsheet would have cut the approvals project. The engine shows it is the key that unlocks the other. The decision is to coordinate the two teams, not to defund one of them.

A new deal arrives. The margin looks good.

Capacity is full. It pushes out business you already had.

Priced for what it displaces, it creates value.

Every deal consumes capacity. With idle capacity nothing is displaced and the floor sits near variable cost. When capacity is full, the deal pushes out existing business, and it must pay for what it displaces. Deal Desk returns the modeled price floor and the incremental value: what the business gains, net of what it gives up.

Each discipline answers a different part of the management question, and the value is in how they connect: question, computed scenario, decision, measured outcome, back into evidence. These are established techniques; we don't claim to have invented them. What is new is wiring them to enterprise goals, deals, evidence and outcome evaluation. Results hold within the stated business model. The statistics layer runs as an optional service; when it is offline, the core profit engine keeps working.
